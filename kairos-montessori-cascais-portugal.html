Kairos Montessori is a small, English-speaking Catholic school founded in 2017 in Quinta da Marinha, Cascais, between Guincho beach and the Sintra hills. It combines the philosophy and materials of the Montessori method with the Cambridge International curriculum, in a Catholic setting “faithful to the Magisterium of the Church”. The school is a non-profit owned by an association (KMCS).

Children move through Montessori three-year cycles: Children’s House (ages 3–6), Lower and Upper Elementary (6–12), then the Lower Adolescent Programme (12–15, following the UK National Curriculum) and the Upper Adolescent Programme (15–18), where students take Cambridge A Levels under the AICE Diploma after a foundation year. The farm is part of everyday learning, and adolescents run it themselves. English is the first language and Portuguese the second.

Frequently asked questions
Is Kairos Montessori a Catholic school?
Yes. It describes itself as a Catholic Montessori school faithful to the Magisterium of the Church, with daily prayer, Mass on Fridays and the Catechesis of the Good Shepherd. Non-Catholic families are welcome.
How much does Kairos Montessori cost?
For 2026/27, tuition is €8,200 – €14,600 a year in Band I and €10,675 – €16,400 in Band II, plus a €2,350 registration fee for new students, €925 annual re-registration and lunch (€1,330 – €1,600 a year).
What curriculum does Kairos follow?
The Montessori method combined with the Cambridge International curriculum: Montessori Children’s House and Elementary, the UK National Curriculum for ages 12–15, and Cambridge A Levels under the AICE Diploma for ages 15–18.
How do admissions work?
Families review the handbook, complete a pre-registration form and visit the school, then send a letter of application. The child spends 2–3 days in class before a decision. Applications by 15 March are answered by 30 March; Children’s House has two admission windows (end of September and end of March).
